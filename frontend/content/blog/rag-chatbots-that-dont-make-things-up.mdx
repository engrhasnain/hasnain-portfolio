Every business that asks me for a chatbot has the same fear: *what if it tells a customer something wrong?* It's the right fear. A chatbot that sounds confident and is wrong is worse than no chatbot at all.

**Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** is the standard fix: before the model answers, you look up the relevant parts of your own documents and give them to it. But RAG on its own isn't magic. Here is what I do on every build to keep answers accurate.

## 1. Clean the data before anything else

Most wrong answers start in the documents, not the model. Old price lists, duplicate policies and PDFs with broken tables all end up in the index if you let them. I spend real time on this step: removing outdated files, fixing extraction, and keeping one source of truth for each topic.

## 2. Chunk by meaning, not by character count

Splitting every document into fixed 500-character pieces cuts ideas in half. I split on headings and sections where possible, keep a small overlap between chunks, and store the document title and section with each chunk so the model knows where text came from.

## 3. Check retrieval separately from generation

If the right passage never reaches the model, no prompt will save the answer. I keep a small set of real questions with known answers and check that retrieval finds the right passages *before* tuning prompts. When retrieval is weak, hybrid search (keywords plus embeddings) or a re-ranking step usually fixes it.

## 4. Tell the model it is allowed to say "I don't know"

The prompt says clearly: answer only from the provided context, and if the answer isn't there, say so and offer a human. Customers forgive "let me connect you with the team". They don't forgive invented refund policies.

## 5. Hand off to a human, and learn from it

Every bot I build has a way out: an email, a live chat or a WhatsApp hand-off. The questions it couldn't answer are the most useful data you have. They show exactly which documents to add next.

## The result

None of this is complicated, but skipping any one step is how chatbots end up making things up. Done together, they give you a bot your team is happy to put in front of customers.
