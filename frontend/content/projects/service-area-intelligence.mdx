## The problem

A contractor's website and ads only work if they target the places the business really serves. Owners describe that loosely: "about 45 minutes out", "not across the river", "the whole county". Turning those answers into a list of towns by hand was slow and different every time. The homepage town matters most. A tiny hamlet cannot carry a homepage, but a small resort town that people search for all the time can, and population by itself does not tell them apart.

## What I built

- **Validated places.** Addresses and towns are geocoded with Google and matched to Google Ads geo targets. The geo target list itself is refreshed from the Google Ads API by a quarterly cron job.
- **A footprint from real drive times.** For each business location, the Routes API measures traffic-aware drive times to every ZIP in range. Exclusions, always-include areas and territory limits, such as a franchise map, are applied on top.
- **Search demand on every place.** Google Ads reach and Keyword Planner search volumes are stored with each town. A new client's top 10 cities are the selected towns with the highest reach.
- **Population with a 3-tier cache.** Population comes from the platform's own cache first, then from city research already done, then from the US Census ACS survey.
- **A rule for the homepage town.** Population and search demand decide whether a town can lead the homepage, or whether the site steps up to a region or a county.
- **Media markets for nationwide clients.** Nationwide plans preview how many city pages, states and media markets they cover, using a county to media-market map.
- **City research.** A FastAPI service builds one research record per city from Census ACS, NOAA climate data, OpenStreetMap, Wikipedia and Tavily, and rebuilds it once its refresh date passes.

Drive times use one standard for every client: the next weekday at 10:00 local time at the business, with the zone taken from the Time Zone API. The homepage rule has clear lines. A town under 15,000 people cannot carry the homepage on its own, unless it gets at least 5,000 monthly searches and at least 8 times its own population in searches. At 40,000 people or more, the town is a confident target. Below the line, the site steps up: first to a named region, then to a searched county-level area, then to "Greater X area" for the nearest well-reached city within 40 miles, then to the county. When the owner names a target, the owner's choice wins.

## Built to be trusted

- **Corrections wait for a person.** Changes captured on a call, such as adding a city or removing a county, become pending rows. Only an explicit approve click writes to the client's geography.
- **Read-back on every save.** After a save, the states, counties and cities actually stored are counted and compared with what the write should have left. Any difference marks the save as failed and lists each problem.
- **Failures are not cached.** A failed population lookup or a ZIP with no route answer is not stored, so it is asked again on the next run.
- **No overwriting a person's choice.** Top cities are picked automatically only for new clients that have none set.
- **One town, one row.** Two records are treated as the same town only when they share a Google place ID and county and one has no Ads target, and the Ads record is kept.

## By the numbers

- 40 API routes across 3 controllers: 35 admin, 3 for service area intake, 2 for the client portal
- 10 data models and 14 test files
- Up to 625 ZIPs measured in one Routes request
- Geo targets refreshed every quarter, on the 1st of January, April, July and October
- 5 public sources in each city research record
