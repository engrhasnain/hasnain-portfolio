## The problem

Real-world events change what people need. A hard freeze brings burst pipes, a hailstorm brings roof checks, and a holiday changes when people book. A post about it a week later is no use, and a post that jumps on the wrong event, like a storm that hurt people, is worse than no post at all. The agency had no reliable way to spot these moments for each client business and respond while they still mattered.

## What I built

- **Feed adapters.** Separate adapters read National Weather Service forecasts, NOAA NCEI storm records, Google Trends and holiday calendars, and turn each one into a common trigger.
- **Client arming rules.** A source is armed for a client only when its feed fits the client's type of business, one of the client's approved services matches a signal the source sends, and the client has a service area for local signals.
- **An exception queue.** A client with missing setup, such as no service area or no approved services, is never armed by default. It goes to a queue for a person to resolve.
- **A 10-stage pipeline.** Each trigger is paired with each angle the client could use, then filtered through rule steps and AI steps until only the pairs that truly change something for that buyer are left.
- **AI where judgement is needed.** Claude scores whether an event really fits a service, writes the line from the owner's own selling point, and checks that any law, credit or rate it mentions is still current.
- **Automatic queueing.** Approved posts are queued on their own, one item per channel, with the timing worked out from the trigger itself.
- **A coverage grid.** Every service is tracked against every content bucket, so gaps in a client's coverage are easy to see.

The pipeline runs in a fixed order. Rule steps handle what rules can decide: geography, season, audience, the safety check, template and duplicate detection, the timing window and the channel list. AI steps handle the parts that need judgement. The last stage writes the result, reads it back and logs the proof. A pair that fails at any stage is still stored with the name of the stage that refused it, so every "no" can be explained later.

## Built to be trusted

- **A kill-list the AI cannot overrule.** A fixed list of terms for deaths, violence, crime, disasters, tragedy and politics runs before any AI step. A kill is final: there is no score to weigh against it and no override.
- **No defaults.** A client with missing setup goes to the exception queue instead of being armed on a guess.
- **Human decisions stay decided.** A later run only rewrites drafts. A post somebody approved keeps its approval and its wording.
- **Refusals are records.** Filtered pairs are kept with their reason and never reach the schedule or the totals.
- **Read-back on every write.** The final stage re-reads what it stored before it counts as done.

- 10 scheduled jobs.
- 10 pipeline stages: 7 rule steps and 3 AI steps (fit, line writing and fact currency).
