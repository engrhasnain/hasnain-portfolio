## The problem

A contractor marketing agency posts to social media for many client businesses at once. For each roofer or plumber, someone has to decide what to post this week, write it, check it and publish it on several platforms. Real-world events change what people need: a hard freeze brings burst pipes, a hailstorm brings roof checks, and a holiday changes when people book. A post about it a week later is no use, and a post that jumps on the wrong event, like a storm that hurt people, is worse than no post at all. One wrong claim or a competitor's name in a caption can cost a client trust. Leads that arrive on Nextdoor wait hours for a reply, and by then the neighbour has called someone else.

## What I built

- **A decision engine.** It scores seven signals for each client: Google Trends, weather, local news, seasonal timing, Search Console performance, engagement history and days since the last post. One strong signal, or two supporting ones, triggers a post.
- **An event engine with arming rules.** Separate adapters read National Weather Service forecasts, NOAA NCEI storm records, Google Trends and holiday calendars, and turn each one into a common trigger. A source is armed for a client only when its feed fits the client's type of business, one of the client's approved services matches a signal the source sends, and the client has a service area for local signals.
- **A 10-stage event pipeline.** Each trigger is paired with each angle the client could use, then filtered until only the pairs that truly change something for that buyer are left. Claude scores whether an event really fits a service, writes the line from the owner's own selling point, and checks that any law, credit or rate it mentions is still current. Approved posts are queued one item per channel, with the timing worked out from the trigger.
- **Six content guards.** Every caption is checked for competitor bleed, competitor names, fluff, unsupported claims, platform rejection risks and AI tells. Each guard can pass it, rewrite one sentence, regenerate the post or block it.
- **Three approval tiers, five platforms.** New clients get full review, established clients get exception-only review, and trusted clients run on autopilot. Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Pinterest and Threads connect through OAuth, and token refreshers keep the LinkedIn, Pinterest and Threads connections from lapsing.
- **Analytics, inbox and a team portal.** Results are collected at 24 hours, 72 hours and 7 days, and Meta inbox, follower and profile sweeps keep the portal up to date. The team gets an AI post generator, a calendar, a publishing queue with approve and reject, scheduled and published views, and an AI reply enhancer with version history and undo.
- **A Nextdoor lead responder.** It spots Nextdoor notifications forwarded by Gmail, opens the thread in a Playwright session per client, and drafts a reply in the owner's voice plus one follow-up.

Both engines run for every client on a schedule. The scoring engine waits for signals to line up, for example a freeze warning in the client's market during pipe repair season, then picks a topic and a format. The event pipeline runs in a fixed order: rule steps handle geography, season, audience, the safety check, template and duplicate detection, the timing window and the channel list, and AI steps handle the parts that need judgement. A pair that fails at any stage is still stored with the name of the stage that refused it, so every "no" can be explained later.

Once a caption is written, the guards run in a fixed order. A small problem gets a targeted rewrite, a bigger one triggers a full regeneration with the violation added as a constraint, and anything serious is blocked for a person. Engagement history never triggers a post on its own: it only changes the format, using the results collected after each post.

## Built to be trusted

- **A kill-list the AI cannot overrule.** A fixed list of terms for deaths, violence, crime, disasters, tragedy and politics runs before any AI step in the event pipeline. A kill is final: there is no score to weigh against it and no override.
- **Hard stops, not guesses.** A guard block stops the post and flags it for human review instead of quietly publishing. A client with missing setup, such as no service area or no approved services, goes to an exception queue instead of being armed on a guess.
- **Autopilot with a limit.** On autopilot, a post that fails a guard is regenerated at most three times, then escalated to a person.
- **Approved stays approved.** A later event run only rewrites drafts, so a post somebody approved keeps its approval and its wording. The final stage re-reads what it stored before it counts as done.
- **Honest neighbourhood replies, no floods.** Validators check every Nextdoor draft, and a rule-based refer-out guard forces a polite referral when a request falls outside the client's trade or credentials, so the AI never promises a job the client can't take. An anti-spam guard caps daily replies per account, never answers the same post twice and adds a random delay before each send. Twilio SMS alerts to the owner stop at 2 per lead.

## By the numbers

- 105 API routes and 12 database models for social publishing, with about 16 scheduled jobs.
- About 203 API routes, about 31 tables and 10 scheduled jobs for event triggers.
- 10 event pipeline stages: 7 rule steps and 3 AI steps (fit, line writing and fact currency).
- 5 platform adapters, 6 content guards and 3 approval tiers.
- The Nextdoor responder adds 23 routes, 8 tables and 3 scheduled jobs.
